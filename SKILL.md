# Write Like FedRAMP

Write practical prose that helps the reader understand a security outcome, a change, or their next action. Default to FedRAMP’s institutional voice. Use Waterman’s public explanations as a secondary influence for conversational material; do not assume every FedRAMP publication is his writing.

## Choose the register

- **Rules and requirements:** Name the responsible party, action or condition, and scope or timing. Keep each obligation independently understandable. Preserve defined terms, exceptions, and the force of MUST, MUST NOT, SHOULD, SHOULD NOT, and MAY. When citing an existing FedRAMP rule, pair its verified identifier with its human-readable name. Keep explanation outside the normative statement. Never strengthen or weaken an obligation merely to improve its style.
- **Announcements and leadership updates:** Lead with the change and practical effect. A milestone story may instead use a brief chronology connecting the original objective, work delivered, and work still ahead. Connect each unmet need to a concrete action, responsible participants, and intended result. Distinguish achievements from plans. Use descriptive headings and lists for parallel actions; contractions fit.
- **Operational notices:** Establish the audience and whether action is required at the start. Explain the reason, then make the required action, timing, conditions, and support route easy to find. Keep exemptions and consequences close to the affected instruction. Preserve qualifiers such as likely, proposed, conditional, and expected; do not turn a target window into a firm deadline. An informational notice can explicitly require no action.
- **Explainers, remarks, and Q&A:** Start with a practical problem and its effect on people doing government work. Make an abstract security idea concrete through an operational event, the evidence it produces, and the decision it informs. One relevant example usually suffices. Prefer candid, everyday language. Remove speech fillers and repetition rather than copying transcript artifacts.

Adapt to the medium. An email does not need the structure of a ruleset or website announcement. Use conversational informality where it fits; formal requirements need precision.

## Apply the voice

Use active verbs and concrete actors: agencies, providers, assessors, and FedRAMP. State what each party does and what useful result follows. Prefer full terms to unnecessary acronyms; define shorthand when repeated long names would obstruct reading. Preserve official defined terms. Where relevant to the supplied facts, emphasize useful evidence, measurable security outcomes, reuse, and practical implementation.

Be candid about constraints, disagreements, and missing evidence. Explain why a plan changed, what follows, and when more information is expected if known. Support urgency with the actual problem and a useful response. Describe iteration through results and feedback when the facts support it; do not invent metrics, certainty, dates, or promises to make an update sound decisive.

Be direct about a problem without insulting the people working within it. Express confidence through a clear explanation and workable next step. Allow warmth without forcing jokes, slogans, exclamation marks, or sales language. Do not reproduce distinctive catchphrases or personal anecdotes to manufacture authenticity.

Preserve the user’s identity and organizational perspective. Use “we” for FedRAMP only when explicitly drafting for FedRAMP. A vendor or advisor should sound like that organization, even when borrowing FedRAMP’s clarity. Do not invent quotations, beliefs, endorsements, or commitments.

## Select live references when useful

For substantial new writing, consult [references/sources.md](references/sources.md) and select up to three style references matching the audience, medium, and purpose. The catalogue offers choices; do not read it as a required reading list. This limit does not restrict factual verification sources. A short rewrite may need only the supplied text and this guidance. User-selected examples take priority.

Use official prose for institutional style. For Waterman’s voice, prefer signed prose or his attributable words in a recording, transcript, or directly quoted interview passage. Separate his words from other speakers, reporters, and surrounding institutional text. Hosts’ summaries, meeting minutes, and event descriptions do not establish his phrasing. Treat a clip or selected quotations as a limited sample; do not claim to have reviewed an entire recording from an article or listing.

This skill is a standalone specialization of the writing-style workflow. When used with write-like-me, the user’s chosen FedRAMP material replaces retrieval of personal correspondence. Keep reference handoffs internal, with only titles and original links, up to three sources. Do not save retrieved prose, transcripts, or a writing-sample cache.

## Preserve factual authority

Style examples are not current policy authority. Verify substantive requirements, dates, certification terminology, and applicability against current official sources when writing about them. For CR26 implementation details, consult the canonical [FedRAMP rules repository](https://github.com/FedRAMP/rules), including definitions and metadata. Check certification type, path, class, stakeholder, effective dates, and transition provisions before generalizing a rule. For operational announcements, also check relevant current public notices and the linked implementation guidance; these may explain timing or decisions not captured in an older rules example.

Do not let an older interview, blog post, preview, or legacy page override current rules. Check status at the relevant section or statement: one published notice can contain a final decision, a future pilot, and draft rules. Distinguish pilot certification from full certification and FedRAMP Certification from an agency's authorization when relevant. Preserve historical context when requested. If sources conflict or verification is unavailable, identify the specific unresolved claim rather than inventing a plausible requirement.

Before delivery, check that the main point and next action are clear, that obligations and qualifications survived the edit, and that the voice fits the actual speaker. Return the requested writing, with factual citations where useful. Append a style analysis only if asked.
