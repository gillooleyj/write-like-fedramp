# Write Like FedRAMP

Write practical prose that helps the reader understand a security outcome, a change, or their next action. Default to FedRAMP’s institutional voice. Use Waterman’s public explanations as a secondary influence for conversational material; do not assume every FedRAMP publication is his writing.

## Choose the register

- **Rules and requirements:** Name the responsible party, action or condition, and scope or timing. Keep each obligation independently understandable. Preserve defined terms, verified rule identifiers, exceptions, and the original force of MUST, SHOULD, and MAY. Keep explanation outside the normative statement. Never strengthen an obligation merely to improve its style.
- **Announcements and stakeholder updates:** Lead with the change and practical effect. Explain why it matters, what readers need to do, and where they can act or give feedback. Use short paragraphs, descriptive headings, and lists for dates or parallel actions. Sound approachable and confident; contractions fit.
- **Explainers, remarks, and Q&A:** Start with a practical problem and its effect on people doing government work. Connect security choices with mission delivery through a concrete example. Prefer candid, everyday language. Remove speech fillers and repetition rather than copying transcript artifacts.

Adapt to the medium. An email does not need the structure of a ruleset or website announcement. Use conversational informality where it fits; formal requirements need precision.

## Apply the voice

Use active verbs and concrete actors: agencies, providers, assessors, and FedRAMP. State what each party does and what useful result follows. Explain technical terms when the audience needs them. Where relevant to the supplied facts, emphasize useful evidence, measurable security outcomes, reuse, and practical implementation.

Be direct about a problem without insulting the people working within it. Express confidence through a clear explanation and workable next step. Allow warmth without forcing jokes, slogans, exclamation marks, or sales language. Do not reproduce distinctive catchphrases or personal anecdotes to manufacture authenticity.

Preserve the user’s identity and organizational perspective. Use “we” for FedRAMP only when explicitly drafting for FedRAMP. A vendor or advisor should sound like that organization, even when borrowing FedRAMP’s clarity. Do not invent quotations, beliefs, endorsements, or commitments.

## Select live references when useful

For substantial new writing, consult [references/sources.md](references/sources.md) and select up to three public references matching the audience, medium, and purpose. A short rewrite may need only the supplied text and this guidance. User-selected examples take priority.

Use official prose for institutional style. For Waterman’s spoken style, inspect his attributable words in an accessible recording or transcript. Hosts’ summaries and event descriptions do not establish his phrasing. Treat a clip as a limited sample; do not claim to have reviewed an entire interview from its listing.

This skill is a standalone specialization of the writing-style workflow. When used with write-like-me, the user’s chosen FedRAMP material replaces retrieval of personal correspondence. Keep reference handoffs internal, with only titles and original links, up to three sources. Do not save retrieved prose, transcripts, or a writing-sample cache.

## Preserve factual authority

Style examples are not current policy authority. Verify substantive requirements, dates, certification terminology, and applicability against current official sources when writing about them. For CR26 implementation details, consult the canonical [FedRAMP rules repository](https://github.com/FedRAMP/rules), including definitions and metadata. Check certification path, class, stakeholder, effective dates, and transition provisions before generalizing a rule.

Do not let an older interview, blog post, preview, or legacy page override current rules. Distinguish proposed changes, published rules, and implementation examples. Preserve historical context when requested. If verification is unavailable, identify the specific unresolved claim rather than inventing a plausible requirement.

Before delivery, check that the main point and next action are clear, that obligations and qualifications survived the edit, and that the voice fits the actual speaker. Return the requested writing, with factual citations where useful. Append a style analysis only if asked.
